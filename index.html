Places come from lyric pages, song-fact sites and press features. Genres come from each artist's Wikipedia page. We've checked what we could, but a few places and first-chart years may still be off, so if you spot a mistake, tell us. Two places count as the same if they are within 25 km of each other, and whole states or countries are ignored. Maps use Natural Earth (public domain). Lyrics are never shown, only place names.

How to play

The idea. Songwriters name real places. Each dot on the map is a place named in one artist's songs. Guess the artist.

Your turn. Type a name and press Guess. You have five tries. Each try gets a row that tells you how close you are:

Genre: green means the same main genre. Amber means different main genres, but the two artists share a genre tag (the small text says which). Genres come from each artist's Wikipedia page.
Era: the decade their first song charted. The arrow points toward the right answer.
Home: where they come from. Green is the same state, amber is the same region.
Shared pins: how many of your guess's places sit within 25 km of a place on this map. Whole states and countries don't count.

Crowded map? Pins that sit close together can be pulled apart. Scroll the mouse wheel, pinch with two fingers, double-click, or press + and − to zoom in. Drag to move the map, and press Reset view to start over. Tap a pin to see its name.

Stuck? Two free hint buttons sit under the guesses:
How many places?: how many places are on the map, and how many are outside the continental US.
Hint about the name: how many words the name has and its first letter.
Tap any pin on the map to see its name, and tap a column title for a plain-words explanation.

Example. You guess Johnny Cash and get green Genre, amber Era with “↑ later”, red Home. So: country, but a later decade, and from somewhere else.
